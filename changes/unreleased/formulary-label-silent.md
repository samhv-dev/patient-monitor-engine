### Changed

- **A dilution the label says nothing about is no longer graded as wrong.** Every fluid that a label-only formulary
  entry does not list now carries the label's reason (`notListed`): the label advises against it (with its section and
  words), names it only for the line the drug is given through, or is silent. Only "advises against" is a wrong diluent
  (a deviation staged, a formulary violation delivered). A silent label reads "The label does not mention this fluid; it
  neither lists it nor advises against it", and a running-line fluid "The label names this fluid only for the line the
  drug is given through, not for mixing": neither is graded. The words are the same on the learner's tray, the
  instructor's card and the Debrief. Intravenous lidocaine lists glucose 5 % on a premixed lidocaine-in-glucose 5 % label
  (Baxter, 4 and 8 mg/mL); saline stays unlisted and reads as silent. A record made before keeps the finding it stored.
