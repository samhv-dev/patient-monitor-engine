### Changed

- **The kidney's resting filtration comes from its own feedback, with a sourced set point and a sourced filtration
  coefficient** (research/44 L2-26). The set point that tubuloglomerular feedback defends is now 125 mL/min (Miller 10e
  ch. 15 p. 334: normal GFR 180 L/day) and Kf is 12.5 mL/min/mmHg (Fig. 15.8: GFR 125 ÷ net filtration pressure 10).
  The resting afferent tone is the feedback's own equilibrium, no longer Pulse's constant afferent resistance. Before,
  that constant set the resting GFR (117 mL/min), and the feedback then defended it. The glomerular capillary pressure
  is now a prediction: 60.0 mmHg at rest, the figure's 60. The filtration fraction is 0.239 and resting renal blood
  flow and urine output are unchanged.
- **The afferent arteriole's maximal dilation (the feedback's lower clamp) is derived from the lower limit of renal
  autoregulation** (parameter tables §5.2 `rblLL`, renal perfusion pressure 80). The plateau's knee therefore sits at
  RPP 80 (MAP 85 at CVP 5). It was Pulse's 2.2 mmHg·s/mL, which put the knee at about RPP 85 with the sourced set
  point. The derived value stands until a direct measurement of maximal afferent dilation replaces it.
- The set point and Kf scale together with the patient's reference renal blood flow, so the resting Starling balance
  is the same at every body weight. Before, the resting GFR barely changed with weight (10 mL/min at 3 kg, 140 at
  127 kg); now it is proportional to weight (5.4 and 227). This body-size rule awaits a ruling.
- "Rest GFR ≈ 125 mL/min" is no longer a known miss.
