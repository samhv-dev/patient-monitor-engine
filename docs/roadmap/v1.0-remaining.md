| Chemoreflex pressor limb through the ONE sympathetic efferent | `chemoFactors` (circ/model.ts) applies the hypercapnic pressor response as its own multiplier on HR and SVR (+1 %/mmHg PaCO2 above 50, capped at +20 %, an [ENG] size) beside the baroreflex: the present term is an independent constrictor with no sympatholysis and the wrong sign. Found 2026-10-27 while gating E134 (the low-flow PI row, then the class IV haemorrhage course, where it is worth ≈ 1 mmHg of MAP at +10 min and holds the patient on the compensated side of his collapse). The sources: hypercapnia LOWERS systemic resistance (≈ 0.4 % of SVR per mmHg, non-sympathetic: Walley 1990 after propranolol; Cullen & Eger 1974 for the intact net, where CO and HR rise and TPR falls) and depresses the myocardium (≈ 4 % of contractility per 10 mmHg PaCO2, ≈ 7 % per 0.1 pH: Walley 1990 Emax), while the sympathetic limb is what raises output; and the chemoreflex is POTENTIATED, not occluded, when the baroreflex is unloaded (several-fold at MAP 40–50: Heistad 1974; the hypercapnic MSNA response survives baroreflex loading: Somers 1991), so there is no shared ceiling that switches it off — the limbs share an EFFERENT. The fix: the chemoreflex sympathetic limb (hypercapnic and hypoxic, sized 4–8 % of baseline MSNA per mmHg from Somers 1989/1991 with the ventilation-related restraint stated) as an afferent of the one sympathetic efferent the baroreflex drives, carrying the sympatholysis and the catecholamine-responsiveness scaling; CO2's direct vasodilation; and the myocardial depression through the existing contractility path, so the net reverses where the inotropic reserve is gone (Karnad 1993: relieving venous hypercarbia in exsanguinated dogs raised cardiac output without volume). Nothing [ENG] | v2.0 workstream 2 (autonomic hub); evidence §2.5 |
| Pressure-line missed feet (ABP, PAP) | The pulse detector (`l3/pulse/detector.ts`) misses some arterial upstrokes, so the numerics (`l3/pressure-numerics/numerics.ts`) measure two or more beats as one. A smaller upstroke after a large one stays under 60 % of the SSF peak average, or the SSF never falls below its re-arm level between beats. Measured 2026-10-27 on main 25771808 (an interval longer than 166 % of the recent median, checked against the engine's beat list). Where it happens, as ABP intervals over the limit: untreated MH 427 of 5669, from about 15 min, at the E133 PVCs; MANUAL AF 150 93 of 1136; the haemorrhage course 11 of 1878 (8 at 560–632 s in low flow, 3 after ROSC); the tamponade showcase 5 of 1670; Ali's low-flow case none. PAP is unmeasured: no run connects a PAP line. What the display reads: over a merged interval SBP is the larger beat's, DBP the last beat's and MAP the mean of the beats. Against the mean of the true ejecting beats, the displayed SBP reads +8.4 mmHg high in AF 150 and +2.4 in MH, and DBP and MAP stay within a few mmHg. The AF 150 row that passes against the displayed line therefore compares the cuff with a line that itself reads high. Mechanism to build: Pan & Tompkins' search-back (1985): an interval over the missed limit is re-scanned at a lower threshold and split at the missed foot. It belongs in the one foot path shared by the pleth and the pressure lines, with #153's pleth own-period rule revisited under the same rule. Spurious feet must be solved first: a half-threshold prototype recovered 108 of 169 missed AF pulses (1 spurious foot), but only 174 of 388 in MH, with 46 spurious feet plus 18 in genuine long beats. Finding the feet also moves the arterial pulse rate. Rejected: extending #153's own-period rule to the pressure lines. It never measures the missed beat (SBP unchanged), and it truncates genuine long diastoles (57 of the 427 flagged intervals in MH, 52 of 179 in the other scenarios): MAP 71 → 83 at the HR step 120 → 40, 65 → 77 in AV block 32, 127 → 145 and 199 → 214 after ROSC. PPV and pulsus paradoxus read the engine's beats, not this detector, and are unaffected. Diagnosis: the project's `research/ibp-missed-foot-diag/` | v1.0 later rc |
